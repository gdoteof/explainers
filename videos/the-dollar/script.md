## pays: Who pays
> Projector: the shorter bar, what came in, moves to the middle and is cut up.

[fact: mts, trustees]
Now the other side: where the dollar comes from. Of every dollar the government collected, fifty-one cents was income tax. Thirty-two was payroll tax, the kind that pays for Social Security.

[fact: mts]

> Wide: two drawings pinned either side of the screen: a few people carrying a crowd, and a crowd carrying a few.

[line]

[fact: taxfoundation]

## well: Spending it well
> Wide: the screen is blank. The host steps forward, large, and holds up the paddle: MY OPINION.

[line]
Which brings me to the thing I actually believe about all this. It is not about which slices we should have.

Spending money is easy. Spending money well is hard. And it is a lot harder to spend two hundred billion dollars well than it is to spend two hundred.

## objection: The strongest reply
> Wide: the host steps aside. The other side's card takes the middle of the screen, as large as the host's opinion was.

[line]
Now the strongest thing anyone can say back to me. Spending well is hard: that is exactly what you would say if you wanted to spend nothing.

[view: cbpp]
Here is that case from people who make it for a living, the Center on Budget and Policy Priorities. Most improper payments, they say, are paperwork problems and honest mistakes with complicated rules.

[view: cbpp]
Fraud, they say, is something else: it means lying on purpose.

[view: cbpp]
More red tape, they say, does not make payments more accurate. Nor does cutting the programme. And nobody counts the opposite error: the people who qualified, and were turned away by a confusing form.

[estimate: census]
And the stakes are large. By the Census Bureau's estimate, Social Security alone kept almost twenty-nine million people out of poverty in 2024.

[line | pause 0.8]
So the objection, at its bluntest. The choice was never between flawed spending and perfect spending. It is between flawed spending and people going without.

> Wide: the host comes back, large, with the paddle: MY OPINION.

<!-- DRAFT. The first beat is his words, from another conversation (about people who could work and do not). The three after it are Claude's and need his answer: this is the one question he has not been asked yet. -->
[opinion]
I think that is right. Somebody getting a little money they should not have does not bother me on principle. On principle, I don't care. What matters is practical.

[opinion]
So for me, hard is not a reason to spend less. It is a reason to ask three things of every slice.

[opinion]
How does the money get there? Who touches it on the way? And how would we know if it worked?

[opinion | pause 0.8]
What would change my mind? Show me a big programme that judges every case one at a time, in a hurry, and still gets it right. Then I will stop saying that is where the money leaks.
